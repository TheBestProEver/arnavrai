Solo founder, current focus

Beacon

A one-button satellite SOS wearable, built end to end. Custom PCB, privacy-first storage and a smart alert escalation algorithm.

Top three and a $4,000 prize at the UChicago Tech Showcase.

Hardware

From dev kit to a 42mm board

I prototyped on a Nordic nRF9160 DK with an iBASIS eSIM, a switch, a piezo buzzer and two LEDs on a breadboard.

The production board was designed in Flux: schematic, 2D layout, a six-layer stack-up and a 3D model, all routed to a circular 42mm outline and checked against the enclosure volume.

Inside, top to bottom: the yellow button, silver trim, halo light pipe, springs, main PCB, battery, charging circuitry and ridged shell.

Design

Engineered to be found by feel

In a high-stress emergency, fine motor skills disappear.

  • Flush surface. The cap sits level with the frame, so nothing in a pocket can press it.
  • A 1mm grip ring. A thumb sliding across finds the boundary instantly, even in the dark.
  • Halo confirmation. A light pipe glows green for 3 seconds once the message reaches the network.

Try it

Hold for 3 seconds, or double-tap

A single accidental bump does nothing, which cuts the false alarms that cause responder fatigue. Press the button on the device, or use these controls.

I’m joining Professor Young-Kee Kim’s group as an undergraduate researcher, developing agentic AI workflows on digital twins of Fermilab’s FAST/IOTA and PIP-II.

The project is part of the Multi-Office Accelerator Team (MOAT) initiative.

Digital twins

A machine and its mirror

A digital twin is a simulation that mirrors a real machine while it runs.

AI agents and controls can be tried on the twin before they touch the real beam. IOTA, the ring this scene is loosely based on, is a small storage ring at Fermilab built for accelerator research.

A stylized storage ring, not to scale. Dipoles bend the gold beam and shed synchrotron light at every bend; the digital twin above tracks its orbit.

Electronics, NASA RockSat

DOLL-E

Dust Observations in Low-gravity and Low-pressure Environments: an experiment to measure the velocity at which colliding dust particles stick together.

It flies inside a NASA RockSat rocket and never leaves it. I work on its electronics and built its particle simulator, which models 12 forces.

The simulator on GitHub

Avionics, NASA CubeSat

PULSE-A

A CubeSat headed for orbit, with launch planned for the second half of 2027.

I’m on the avionics team, designing PCBs in KiCad and the laser driver electronics.

A stylized flight: liftoff, an x-ray of the DOLL-E payload inside the rocket, then stage separation, a 3U CubeSat leaving its dispenser, and a laser link to Earth.
